# Creative-Elites Led Regeneration in Songwat Old Neighborhood, Bangkok

## Overview
This project summarizes my master’s thesis on how creative-led regeneration has unfolded in Bangkok’s Songwat Old Neighborhood, a former Chinese-Thai wholesale corridor that has become a riverside creative-heritage area.[file:86]

I analyse who initiated regeneration, which personal, economic, and spatial factors shaped their roles, and how collaboration between key stakeholders – including creative entrepreneurs, family-business successors, members of the Made in Songwat collective, and officials from the Bangkok Metropolitan Administration (BMA) and the Creative Economy Agency (CEA) – drives change in Songwat.[file:86]

## Data and Methods
- **Design:** Qualitative single-case study.[file:86]  
- **Data sources:**
  - Semi-structured interviews with creative entrepreneurs, family-business heirs, Made in Songwat members, BMA officers, and CEA staff.[file:86]
  - Field observations during ordinary weekdays and weekends, as well as major events such as *Awakening Songwat*.[file:86]
  - Documentary sources including BMA’s 20-year development plan, CEA policy documents, media reports, awards, and rankings that frame Songwat as a creative-heritage district.[file:86]
- **Analysis:**
  - Thematic coding of interview transcripts, observation notes, and documents using an open-coding approach that combined deductive (theory-led) and inductive (data-led) codes.[file:86]
  - Comparison of themes across actor groups (creative elites, family businesses, municipal officers, CEA staff) to trace networks, collaboration patterns, and perceived impacts.[file:86]
  - Constant comparison between micro-level narratives and macro-level policy and development processes to explain how creative elites, government agencies, and local networks jointly shape regeneration in Songwat.[file:86]

## Analytical Framework
The analysis connects three levels:[file:86]

- **Actors:** Creative elites, family-business successors, grassroots networks such as Made in Songwat, and state agencies like BMA and CEA.[file:86]
- **Mechanisms:** Placemaking practices, adaptive reuse of historic shophouses into cafés, galleries, boutique stays, and hybrid family-business spaces; branding strategies; and elite networking across business and policy fields.[file:86]
- **Outcomes:** Upgrading of the built environment and diversification of the local economy, alongside tensions around unequal visibility, rising land values, and the risks of large corporate-led riverside megaprojects.[file:86]

## Translating Qualitative Data into Indicators
Although the project is qualitative, I translated key themes into simple, data-friendly indicators to support visual analysis:

- Number and types of interviewees by actor group (creative entrepreneur, family-business heir, Made in Songwat member, BMA, CEA).[file:86]
- Frequency counts of major themes (e.g. collaboration, heritage reuse, placemaking, benefits of regeneration, problems such as displacement or speculation, megaproject risks) across actor groups.[file:86]
- Timelines of key events – such as the first creative openings, Songwat Week, Bangkok Design Week participation, and major brand collaborations – that increased visibility and policy attention to Songwat over time.[file:86]
- Simple maps showing the concentration of creative businesses, adaptive reuse projects, and elite-owned properties along Songwat Road.[file:86]

To build these indicators, I organised coded segments from interviews and fieldnotes into Excel matrices (actor × theme × perceived impact), then produced charts and annotated maps to highlight patterns in who drives regeneration, how they collaborate, and who benefits.[file:86]

## What This Project Demonstrates
This project demonstrates my ability to:

- Structure messy qualitative material (interviews, observations, documents) into systematic datasets that can be summarised through indicators, timelines, and maps.[file:86]
- Connect neighbourhood-level practices with wider debates on creative cities, placemaking, and creative elites in contemporary Bangkok.[file:86]
- Communicate complex urban-regeneration processes through clear visual and written explanations suitable for planners, policymakers, and non-academic audiences.[file:86]
